THE ENGINEERING BEHIND VOCULUS

Built with technology.
Driven by innovation.

VOCULUS combines programming, electronics and mechanical engineering to create a calculator controlled through spoken mathematical instructions.

01 / SOFTWARE

The intelligence of the system.

Software converts a spoken instruction into a mathematical result that the hardware can understand.

SOFTWARE COMPONENTS
🐍

Python

Python handles the recognized mathematical instruction, performs arithmetic operations and prepares the result for transmission.

🎙

Speech Recognition

Speech recognition converts spoken words into text so that the computer can interpret the mathematical instruction.

↔

Serial Communication

Serial communication transfers the calculated result from the computer to the Arduino controller.

02 / HARDWARE

The machinery behind the answer.

Electronic and mechanical components work together to create the physical display.

HARDWARE COMPONENTS
⚡

Arduino Mega

The microcontroller receives the result and controls the servo positions required to form the displayed digits.

⚙

14 Servo Motors

Each of the two digits contains seven individually controlled physical segments, operated by servo motors.

🔋

Power Supply

The servo system uses an appropriately rated external power source, while the Arduino is powered separately through USB.

🔌

Wiring System

Signal, power and ground connections allow the controller and servo motors to work together.

◈

Physical Segments

Seven movable segments combine to form each digit, creating a mechanical seven-segment-style display.

▣

Structural Base

The base supports the components and helps maintain the alignment of the two-digit display.

04 / MECHANICAL ENGINEERING

Fourteen motors. Two digits.

The display uses movable physical segments instead of a conventional electronic LED display.

Mechanical digit formation

Each digit consists of seven individual segments. Each segment is positioned by its own servo motor.

For two digits, the system uses 14 servo motors in total.

The Arduino sends control signals to position the segments and form the required numerical display.
